# Writing DNA: scope and evidence

Analyze each document family/language separately. Count paragraph/sentence lengths, headings, script distribution and sentence endings. Interpret politeness, risk phrasing and terminology with actual representative passages and the original audience context.

Every rule must include `language`, `document_family`, `source_ids`, `status` (`observed` or `suggested`) and `confidence`. Compare conflicting samples; do not invent a universal company voice from one brochure. Verify facts and technical terms independently.

Store raw examples in the portable Company Pack only if source rights and public disclosure permit. Private documents may inform redacted aggregate rules in an internal workspace, but private text and unreviewed claims must not leak into public artifacts.
