# Evidence and source conflicts

Inspect actual relevant source content and selected images, not just filenames or inventories. Follow explicit source priority; a newer filename alone does not prove approval. Preserve exceptions, date, geography, population, units and claim strength when paraphrasing.

Use a compact source record only where facts need tracing: claim/fact, source and page/location, date/version, scope, status and affected section. Reuse the document's existing content source rather than maintaining parallel ledgers.

Before inserting a supplied image, inspect the source page and final crop for the actual brand/object, relevance, date/version and proposal-versus-completed status. Mixed-brand or outdated imagery is not made relevant by its filename. Keep the useful caption and source location; a newer user-supplied design replaces older views only within its actual scope.

When sources conflict, resolve from explicit instructions or evidence. If the answer materially changes the result, ask one concrete question and work on unaffected parts. Leave unsupported values unknown; do not invent a target or ranking to complete a chart. User opinions can guide emphasis and recommendations but do not become verified facts.

For volatile claims, verify authoritative current sources for the intended publication date. Cached files and previous approvals do not establish current availability, prices or operating conditions. Stop research when evidence is sufficient for the actual claim; widen research only for unresolved material gaps.

Distinguish confirmed fact, supported interpretation, proposal and illustrative concept. Show useful conditions in reader-facing language, keep internal evidence-management labels in working notes, and include required attribution near the claim.

For an actual research gap, use [research strategy](research-strategy.md) to choose depth, tools and stopping criteria. Research is conditional, not a prerequisite for every edit. Trace material external claims and chart inputs to opened sources, including the relevant page/table/section and date or period. Search snippets and generated research summaries are discovery aids. Place useful citations and limits inside the delivered file, not only in the chat response. For computed values, preserve the source inputs and calculation basis.
