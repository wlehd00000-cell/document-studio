# Writing inside reports, proposals, plans and presentations

Write directly in the document's final language. Use the primary reader, document purpose, reading situation and desired decision/action from the brief. Organize the argument and evidence before polishing sentences. A faithful edit or translation retains the accepted meaning, structure and degree of certainty unless the user asks to change them.

This is the prose reference for [document writing](../../document-writing/SKILL.md). Reuse the accepted architecture and one canonical manuscript; do not independently redo intake, research or the full document's argument for a local wording change.

## Korean DEMO report voice

Korean reading reports default to [source-backed DEMO ESG prose](../../document-writing/references/demo-esg-writing.md) and [natural long-form reasoning](../../document-writing/references/demo-esg-korean-longform.md) unless the user or accepted template chooses another voice. Choose the relevant company profile and paragraph function; preserve the new document's facts. Sufficient connected explanation is essential: an activity list with formal endings does not reproduce the source reports' logic. Use [Korean line-flow criteria](../../document-writing/references/demo-esg-korean-lineflow.md) after layout and whenever copy, font, width or export changes.

## Document prose

- Reports need supported findings and their implications. Proposals need a clear idea, why it serves the need, and how it can work. Plans need identifiable actions, owners, conditions and sequence. Presentation copy must work at the actual viewing distance and pace.
- Use the user's selected paragraph and actual reference document as the voice model. Preserve formal register where appropriate. Concrete subjects and verbs clarify responsibility; parallel noun phrases can work in tables. Chat concision is not a quota for report length: include the explanation the reader needs, and remove repetition rather than evidence.
- Keep necessary explanation in connected paragraphs. Do not reduce every document to fragments, bullets or catchphrases. A supported takeaway headline can guide a presentation; factual headings often suit official plans.
- Draft a caption or short interpretation for a chart when it adds meaning. Explain the relevant difference, trend or implication; state when a pattern is only descriptive or a conclusion remains conditional. Do not simply repeat every visible number. Keep conditions beside the claim and distinguish source evidence, interpretation and proposal.
- For Korean or Japanese translation/polishing, use only the target module: [business Korean](../../business-korean-translation/SKILL.md) or [business Japanese](../../business-japanese-translation/SKILL.md). They also inform natural drafting from supplied facts. For long or consequential bilingual translation, use [translation quality](translation-quality.md); do not load both languages for a single-language file.
- For bilingual files, share fact/section IDs when useful and align claims and status. Differences in tone or explanatory depth do not authorize different commitments.

## One integrated edit

Paragraph boundaries and rendered line boundaries are different decisions. For layout or wrapping revisions use [text flow](text-flow-and-wrapping.md); do not treat automatic wrapping or a clean punctuation check as editorial approval. Re-render affected text after register or length changes.

Review factual equivalence, argument/order, sentence relationships, tone and concision together. Revisit only passages needing revision. No mandatory humanizing pass, change percentage or stock-word score.

Read titles alone for the argument, then the body for sufficient explanation. Layout shortening must preserve the qualifications that make a statement true. Keep the preview and final file bound to the same corrected text source.

A requested revision ends with the updated document file. A requested content-only planning stage may stop at its agreed outline, but do not treat that as delivery of a finished report or presentation.
